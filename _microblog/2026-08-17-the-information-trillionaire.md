Information is the fundamental currency of the world.
It is the unit of exchange underlying money, relationships and energy.
Dollars, in all their capitalistic might, abstract away much richer information about whatever is being sold or purchased. "A bottle of wine aged for 10 years in Venice" can be compressed down to a $100 figure. A single number that encodes something about the effort of the seller and the demand of the consumer.
In relationships, years of shared experiences gets averaged into a single feeling between two people.
And even in physics, something as fundamental as energy is an abstraction of the richer context of the system's state and history.
This is all to say that underneath all the abstractions we deal with on daily basis, information --for all the good and the bad-- is the ground truth.

To that end, i romanticise about becoming rich with information. An information trillionaire if you will.

**II. What is information wealth?**  
Unlike with money, information richness is tricky to define. For all you know, some tic-tock kid sponging up bits -- scroll by scroll --  is the wealthiest mind in town. Yet, this would be like calling someone rich based solely on the money flowing into a pocket with a hole in it. Little, if any, of that scrolling is actually absorbed.

Taking the opposite extreme, a world memory champion wouldn't be an information trillionaire either. A mind filled with perfectly preserved facts can still be informationally poor if those facts have low utility; akin to a weak currency.

Information becomes wealth when it can be **compressed, connected and deployed**.
Compression is the ability to turn thousands of observations into a smaller number of useful models. Connection is the ability to recognize that something learned in one domain explains something apparently unrelated in another. Deployment is the ability to retrieve the right model at the right moment and use it to predict, decide, create, or act.

So perhaps information wealth is not measured in bits accumulated, but in **bits made useful**.

The TikTok addict has enormous informational income but terrible savings. The memory champion has an impressive vault, but the assets inside it may produce little return. The information trillionaire, by contrast, owns a dense network of models whose value compounds as each new piece of information finds its place among the old.

sufficiently good information makes subsequent information cheaper to acquire. Once you understand probability, a hundred observations about uncertainty collapse into a handful of principles. Once you understand evolution, thousands of biological facts stop being isolated facts. Once you understand incentives, seemingly unrelated behaviours in markets, politics, companies, and relationships begin to rhym.

The richest mind, then, is not the one that has seen the most. It is the one for whom **each new thing seen illuminates the greatest number of things already known**.

That is the kind of wealth I romanticise accumulating.
