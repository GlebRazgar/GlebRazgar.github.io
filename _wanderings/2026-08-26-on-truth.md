What is truth?  
My current understanding of truth comes from Physics, and Kung-Fu Panda.  
Physics gives me the intuition that truth is an objective fact, merely discovered by observation.  
Whilst Kung-Fu Panda gives me the opposite intuition, that some truths depend on belief. That while the Secret Ingridient soup has no 'secret ingredient' at all, the actual 'secret ingredient' is that everyone believes there is one.

These are quite contradictory versions of truth. And whilst the human world seems to operate on both just fine, I would like to grapple with the philosophy of truth a little more rigorously to understand what it is, and what it isn't.  
So, I am planning to use this piece to create a map of the major philosophical theories on truth, and then interrogate them. All in hopes of learning wether physics of Kung-Fu Panda is in the right.

get a map of the major positions on truth and then interrogate them.

Some of the questions leading my wondering are:  
Does truth exist? if yes, what makes it so?  
Are there truths that cannot be proven?  
Is truth independent of belief?  
What is the relationship between truth and evidence?  
What is the relationship between truth and consensus? Can something be true for one person and false for another?  
Is objective truth possible for human beings to access?

### **I. Review of theories:**
My map of philosophical truth theories will be based on the STE's (Stanford's Encyclapedia of Philossopy) body of work on Truth, since it looks to cover the main beliefs.

My Sketchpad:
[1. The neo-classical theories of truth](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/truth/#NeoClaTheTru)
		core theories of 20th century are: correspondance, coherence, and pragmatist theories. they are trying to answer the nature of things. what is the nature of truth. Taking the question at face value by saing that there are truths, and the question to be answered concerns their nature.
		In answering this question, each theory makes the notion of truth part of a more thoroughgoing metaphysics or epistemology. Explaining the nature of truth becomes an application of some metaphysical system, and truth inherits significant metaphysical presuppositions along the way.
